# 💡 Problem Statement

In busy hospitals, doctors spend valuable consultation time collecting patient history and reviewing scattered physical medical records. Patients may also face language and literacy barriers while communicating their symptoms and medical history.

This creates a need for an intelligent patient case-taking system that can collect and organize relevant patient information before consultation.

---

# 💡 Proposed Solution

## AI-Powered Multilingual Patient Case-Taking System

Our solution collects patient information through **voice and touch** in their preferred language. It can also scan medical documents and combine them with patient responses.

AI processes the collected information to generate a **structured medical history summary**, which can be accessed by doctors through a dashboard before consultation.

---

# ✨ Key Features

### 🗣️ AI-Powered Patient Interaction

Communicates naturally with patients and supports interaction in their preferred language.

### 🎙️ Voice + Touch Interaction

Patients can provide information through voice or touchscreen interaction.

### 📄 Smart Document Integration

Scans existing prescriptions, laboratory reports, and other medical documents and combines them with patient responses.

### 🔍 OCR-Based Document Processing

Extracts information from scanned medical documents and converts physical records into digital information.

### 🤖 AI-Based Medical Summarization

Combines patient responses and scanned documents to generate a structured medical history summary.

### 👨‍⚕️ Doctor Dashboard

Allows doctors to access patient details, summaries, and scanned documents before consultation.

### 🖥️ Connected Patient Stations

Connects multiple patient displays and scanners through a centralized software system.

### 🧑‍💼 Digital Receptionist

Assists with basic patient registration and information collection, reducing repetitive manual work.

### 📋 Pre-Consultation Information

Makes relevant patient history available to the doctor before the consultation begins.
